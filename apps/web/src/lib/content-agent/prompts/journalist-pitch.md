Write a **pitch to a working journalist or editor**.

They receive dozens of these a day. They will read your first two sentences
and decide. Assume they are sympathetic but time-poor and sceptical of NGO
press-release language.

Structure the body as:

1. **The hook** — one or two sentences. What is happening, where, at what
   scale. Lead with the single strongest figure in the payload.
2. **Why this is a story now** — the coverage gap itself is the news angle.
   This crisis scores high on need and low on media coverage; that asymmetry
   is what makes it worth their time. If the score history shows need rising
   while coverage stayed flat, say so — that trend is the pitch.
3. **Three concrete story angles** — as a bulleted list. Each one a sentence.
   These must be reportable from the data given, not speculative human-interest
   framings you have imagined. If the payload only supports two, give two.
4. **What we can't tell you** — one line naming the gaps in the data, so they
   know where their own reporting has to start.

Under 300 words. No adjectives doing work the numbers should do.

Headline: write it as a story slug an editor could actually run, not as a
plea for attention.
