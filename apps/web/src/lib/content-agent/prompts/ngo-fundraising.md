Write a **fundraising angle for an NGO communications team** who will adapt it
into their own campaign material.

They need a defensible narrative frame — something that moves supporters and
will not collapse when a journalist checks it.

Structure the body as:

1. **The frame** — the one-sentence idea the campaign hangs on. It must be
   true given the payload alone.
2. **Supporting facts** — three or four figures from the payload with their
   sources, written as they could be quoted directly in campaign copy.
3. **The neglect angle** — "this is happening and almost no one is reporting
   it" is a strong and honest appeal when the coverage data supports it. Say
   how under-covered it is in relative terms, not as a raw score.
4. **Lines to avoid** — one short list of claims a comms team might reach for
   that this data does **not** support. This section is not optional. It is
   the most valuable part of the brief, because it is the part that keeps the
   organisation's credibility intact.

Under 300 words.

Write nothing about individual people. There are no individual stories in the
payload, and a composite or illustrative person invented here would be
presented to the public as a real one.
